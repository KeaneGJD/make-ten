I think progressively teaching mechanics is much better than giving the player + − × ÷ immediately. The campaign could evolve roughly like this: Levels Mechanics Example 1–5 ➕ Addition 7 + 3 = 10 6–10 ➕ Addition + bigger targets 11 + 7 = 18 11–15 ➕ Three-number addition 8 + 5 + 7 = 20 16–20 ➖ Subtraction introduced 17 − 7 = 10 21–25 ➕ ➖ Mixed 12 + 7 − 4 = 15 26–30 ✖️ Multiplication introduced 6 × 3 = 18 31–35 ➕ ➖ ✖️ Mixed More possibilities 36+ Special mechanics 🔒 💣 🧊 ⭐ etc. The important part is that old mechanics don't disappear. The player's toolbox grows. So when subtraction is first introduced, we might explicitly say: NEW! SUBTRACTION ➖ You can now subtract numbers to hit the target. Then the first subtraction level could be extremely obvious: MAKE 10 [ 14 ] [ 4 ] Tap 14, tap −, tap 4. But later: MAKE 18 [ 7 ] [ 4 ] [ 5 ] [ 23 ] ... Now the player might see: 23 − 5 = 18 or 7 + 4 + 7 = 18 Eventually, the interesting part becomes finding the best solution among several possibilities, rather than simply spotting two complementary numbers. One change I'd make to our current 15 levels Rather than cramming subtraction into what we already have, I'd keep Levels 1–15 exactly as our introductory addition chapter. Then: Levels 16–20 = Subtraction chapter That gives the game a nice rhythm: 1–10: learn addition and changing targets 11–15: master three-number addition 16: 🎉 NEW MECHANIC — SUBTRACTION And later we can have little chapter markers: CHAPTER 1 — Add It Up Levels 1–15 CHAPTER 2 — Take It Away Levels 16–25 CHAPTER 3 — Multiply Levels 26–35 CHAPTER 4 — Chaos Mixed operators + special tiles That also gives us a much better progression/reward loop for a mobile game. Getting to Level 16 doesn't just mean another harder number — the player actually unlocks something new. So yes: that's exactly how I'd build it.
